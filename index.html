Know what your house is about to cost you.

HomeHQ is a private record of everything in your home — and it uses that record to tell you what is coming before it arrives.

You enter your water heater once. Later, HomeHQ opens to: “Water heater — 13 years old, 3 years past the typical 10-year life.”

Nobody told you that. It came from a date you typed once and a published service life for that kind of equipment. That is the whole idea: the record you keep should work for you, not just sit there.

What it costs to not know

The usual answer to this problem is a home warranty. The average US home warranty runs about $876 a year, plus roughly $108 every time you call someone out. In the one state that publishes loss ratios, those plans pay back about 46¢ on the premium dollar.

HomeHQ does not fix your water heater. It tells you the water heater is the thing to think about this year — which is the part the $876 was supposed to buy.

Why not one of the free ones

Most home apps are free because a contractor pays for the lead. That app has a commercial interest in whether you hire someone. HomeHQ does not, and cannot — there are no leads, no referral fees, and no ads. When it helps you find a plumber, it opens whichever maps app you choose with a trade and your city; never your street address, and nobody pays for the click.

That matters more than it sounds. The last well-funded product in this category shut down and let its domain lapse; the address where people's home records used to live now serves a sportsbook. A subscription you pay for is a business model that has to keep your records to keep you.
